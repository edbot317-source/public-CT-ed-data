Where the rebuild reproduces the state's calculation

For each town-year, the enacted-policy rebuild is run from the actual prior-year entitlement and compared with what CSDE published. exact means within $1, close within 0.5%, not reproduced beyond that. Counterfactual scenarios inherit whatever error the rebuild has for that town and year.

Assumptions and limits

  • Formula. Fully funded grant = need students × foundation × final base aid ratio + regional-district bonus + endowed-academy bonus. Need students = resident students + (FRPL weight × FRPL-eligible) + (concentrated-poverty weight × FRPL students above the threshold share of residents) + (ELL weight × English learners). Base aid ratio = max(1 − [property share × ENGL factor + income share × MHI factor], minimum), plus the Public Investment Community bonus. Factors divide a town's ENGL per capita and MHI by the statewide town median × the threshold factor.
  • Inputs are held fixed. Resident, FRPL and ELL counts, ENGL, population, MHI, PIC bonus points and the two bonuses are taken from each year's calculation worksheet exactly as the state used them. Changing a parameter never changes behavior, enrollment, or property values.
  • Statewide medians are recomputed from the 169 towns only when a slider changes the wealth threshold factor; the medians themselves equal the state's worksheet values in every year.
  • Phase-in follows the schedule actually applied each year: underfunded towns received 16.67% of their gap in FY2023, 20% in FY2024, 56.5% in FY2025 and 100% from FY2026; overfunded towns lost nothing in these years because the budget acts held them harmless. The speed slider scales these shares, capped at 100%.
  • Counterfactual chaining. Scenarios start from every town's actual FY2022 entitlement and then chain year to year on the simulated prior-year grant, so a change compounds through the phase-in. The enacted-policy line is chained the same way, which is why it can differ slightly from CSDE's published line.
  • Hold-harmless is the greater of the computed grant, the prior year, and the FY2017 base. Enacted policy applies it to Alliance and Priority School Districts, using each year's membership list (33 towns in FY2022, 36 from FY2023 when Enfield, Plainfield and Stratford were added). The control can switch it off for every district or extend it to every town.
  • Start year. Years before the chosen start year follow enacted policy exactly; from the start year on, the scenario applies and chains forward on its own simulated prior-year grant.
  • Per-student chart. The student measure is the ECS resident-student count (students the town is fiscally responsible for on October 1), because it is the denominator the formula itself uses and it exists for every town and year; it excludes charter and technical-high-school students. The low-income share is FRPL-eligible students over resident students from the same worksheet. The fitted line is ordinary least squares across the 169 towns, unweighted unless the box is ticked; the slope is reported per 10-percentage-point rise in the FRPL share.
  • Total spending per student (NCEP). The second vertical-axis option uses CSDE's net current expenditures per pupil: all-source current spending (net of tuition revenue, capital, debt service and reimbursable transportation) divided by average daily membership. ADM is the resident-student count, the same count the ECS formula uses, so the denominator is consistent in construction. The two are one October apart: the FY2025 NCEP divides FY2025 spending by the October 2024 count, while the FY2025 ECS grant was computed on the October 2023 count (FY2025 ADM equals the FY2026 ECS resident count to the cent for 135 of 148 towns). Under a scenario, the town's spending is moved by its ECS change one for one, which assumes no offsetting local tax response. NCEP exists only for the 148 towns with a local board (regional-district members carry their tuition payments, not the region's spending) and is published through FY2025; FY2023 onward comes from CSDE's reports as archived by the School and State Finance Project, with the current year from CSDE's own file.
  • Achievement (SEDA). Scores are the Stanford Education Data Archive 2025.2 administrative-district estimates (Reardon, Fahle, Ho, Shear, Saliba, Min, Shim and Kalogrides, 2026), grades 3-8 pooled, empirical-Bayes means on the grade-cohort-standardized (GCS) scale, minus the grade center of 5.5. On that scale the national average of the 2009-2015 cohorts in grade g equals g, so the value plotted is grade levels above or below that national benchmark, not the 2025 national average. SEDA advises the GCS scale for reporting rather than research use. Town boards are matched by name; the 21 towns whose grades 3-8 are run by a K-12 regional district (Regions 10, 12-18 and 20; Region 6 before 2024-25) carry the region's score and are drawn hollow; small districts that SEDA suppresses have no score. Charter and magnet districts are not assigned to towns. When a scenario is active the chart also draws scenario points and a second fitted line: each town's observed score plus the simulated change from the test-score simulation below, so the slope change reflects the simulated policy effect, not an estimated relationship.
  • Test-score simulation. Follows the simulation instructions of 2026-09-14. The shock is the scenario's ECS entitlement minus enacted policy, per ECS resident student, in each fiscal year from the chosen start year, deflated to 2018 dollars with CPI-U and treated as an operating (noncapital) spending increase spent one for one. Effect size β = 0.0343 student-level SD per $1,000 per pupil at four years of exposure, the noncapital marginal effect in Jackson & Mackevicius (2024, AEJ: Applied 16(1), Table 3 col 2). Exposure assumes no churn since kindergarten: min(policy year, grade + 1); the dose rises linearly to four years and is flat after, so with a phased-in shock the effect is β/4 times the sum of the last min(exposure, 4) years' increases. SD are converted to grade levels with k taken from SEDA's own scale definitions: k = national NAEP SD for the grade and subject ÷ the per-grade NAEP growth for the subject, from Table 9 of the SEDA 2025.2 documentation averaged over the four reference cohorts (grade 3 math 2.70 to grade 8 math 3.71, reading 3.30–3.46; SEDA's rule of thumb is about 3). The slope of GCS on CS across all U.S. districts reproduces these to within 0.006. Baselines and weights are SEDA 2025.2 grade-by-subject means averaged over spring 2023–2025. Because the dashboard runs only through FY2025, no cohort reaches the four-year plateau. Statewide and tercile figures are test-weighted; terciles are by FY2025 FRPL share. The Option A sensitivity scales β by the district's FRPL share using the low-income (0.0312) and non-low-income (0.0161) effects relative to the 0.0359 constant (Table 3 col 3) and is not normalized to average one. This is a partial-equilibrium projection with no behavioral responses beyond those embedded in the reduced-form estimates, and no uncertainty is shown. A Python reference implementation with unit tests, an aggregation check of the grade-level files against the pooled estimates, and a log of every run live in the working repository under output/ecs/dashboard/sim and output/ecs/sim.
  • Average household income is an option, not the statute. It substitutes the ACS 5-year mean household income (subject table S1901, same vintage as the median the state used) for the town's median, and replaces the statewide median of medians with the statewide median of town means, so the wealth threshold is rebuilt the same way. Towns with no published mean keep their median.
  • Inflation preset grows the $11,525 foundation from calendar 2013 (the year it was set for FY2014) by the CPI-U annual average of the calendar year before each fiscal year; FY2027 uses the last complete year available. The $13,087 preset applies a 2027 figure to every year, which overstates it for earlier years.
  • FY2023 and FY2027 worksheets are the School and State Finance Project's projections rather than CSDE's final runs: FY2023 was built on preliminary October counts, and FY2027 awaits CSDE's final calculation. The reproduction flags show where that matters.
  • Not modeled: minimum budget requirement interactions, Alliance set-asides (which change who receives the money, not the entitlement), supplemental education aid, special-education adjustments, and any statutory earmark or one-off grant outside the formula.
  • Real dollars. Every dollar figure on the page is in constant 2025 dollars: fiscal year t is deflated with the CPI-U annual average for calendar year t, the convention of the public data release. FY2026 and FY2027 use the 2025 index because later annual averages are not yet complete, so they appear at 2025 prices. The formula itself runs in nominal dollars, so the foundation slider and the $11,525 and $13,087 presets are nominal amounts; the inflation preset raises the nominal foundation with CPI-U from 2013.
  • Data. Amounts: CSDE Bureau of Fiscal Services entitlement files. Inputs: Office of Fiscal Analysis calculation shells as republished by the School and State Finance Project, validated against OPM's equalized net grand list, Census ACS median income and CSDE's published entitlements. Series start in FY2023: earlier worksheets are either incomplete (FY2018–FY2019), use the pre-2022 formula, or were recalculated after the fact (FY2020–FY2021), so only FY2023 onward is used. The FY2022 grant enters only as each town's starting prior-year amount.
Data assembled September 2026 from the public-CT-ed-data release (datasets town_year_ecs_entitlement, town_year_ecs_inputs, ecs_formula_parameters). Town boundaries: Census Bureau cartographic boundary files, 2023.
