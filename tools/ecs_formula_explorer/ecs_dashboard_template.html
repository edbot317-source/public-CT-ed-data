Where the rebuild reproduces the state's calculation

For each town-year, the enacted-policy rebuild is run from the actual prior-year entitlement and compared with what CSDE published. exact means within $1, near-exact within 0.5%, not reproduced beyond that. Counterfactual scenarios inherit whatever error the rebuild has for that town and year.

Assumptions and limits

  • Formula. Fully funded grant = need students × foundation × final base aid ratio + regional-district bonus + endowed-academy bonus. Need students = resident students + (FRPL weight × FRPL-eligible) + (concentrated-poverty weight × FRPL students above the threshold share of residents) + (ELL weight × English learners). Base aid ratio = max(1 − [property share × ENGL factor + income share × MHI factor], minimum), plus the Public Investment Community bonus. Factors divide a town's ENGL per capita and MHI by the statewide town median × the threshold factor.
  • Inputs are held fixed. Resident, FRPL and ELL counts, ENGL, population, MHI, PIC bonus points and the two bonuses are taken from each year's calculation worksheet exactly as the state used them. Changing a parameter never changes behavior, enrollment, or property values.
  • Statewide medians are recomputed from the 169 towns only when a slider changes the wealth threshold factor; the medians themselves equal the state's worksheet values in every year.
  • Phase-in follows the rule actually applied each year, verified against CSDE's published grants. FY2019 (PA 17-2) moved each town from its FY2017 grant by 4.1% of the gap to full funding if underfunded and 25% if overfunded. FY2020–FY2022 moved from the prior year by 10.66% (underfunded) or 8.33% (overfunded) of the gap, with the gap still measured against the FY2017 grant; from FY2022 overfunded towns were held harmless. FY2023 on moves from the prior year by 16.67%, 20%, 56.5%, 100% and 100% of the gap against the prior year, overfunded towns held harmless. The speed slider scales these shares, capped at 100%. Modeling choice: the engine never lets a phase-in overshoot the fully funded grant, so a town moving up stops at full funding and a town moving down stops there too; the statute does not need such a cap and it never binds under enacted rules, but it keeps the speed slider well behaved above 1×.
  • Counterfactual chaining. Scenarios start from every town's actual FY2017 grant, the statutory base, and then chain year to year on the simulated prior-year grant, so a change compounds through the phase-in. The enacted-policy line is chained the same way, which is why it can differ slightly from CSDE's published line. The largest such gap is real rather than a modeling error: CSDE recalculated the FY2020 grants of nine Alliance towns with revised Public Investment Community rankings after the worksheet was built, so the chained enacted line for those towns (Bristol, at about $345,000, is the largest) sits off the published line in FY2020 and FY2021 and rejoins it in FY2022, when the worksheet's prior-year column is CSDE's final figure.
  • Hold-harmless means the grant never falls below the starting point of that year's phase-in (the prior year; the FY2017 grant in FY2019) and, from FY2024, never below the FY2017 grant either (CSDE did not apply the FY2017 floor in FY2023 to the three towns added to the Alliance list that year). Enacted policy applies it to Alliance and Priority School Districts, using each year's membership list (33 towns through FY2022, 36 from FY2023 when Enfield, Plainfield and Stratford were added). The control can switch it off for every district or extend it to every town.
  • Need weights before FY2022. Through FY2021 the enacted formula used PA 17-2's need weights: concentrated poverty counted FRPL students above 75% of residents at 5%, and English learners at 15%; the 60% / 15% / 25% weights enacted by PA 21-2 (June Special Session §§384-386) apply from FY2022. A slider left at its default applies each year's enacted weight, so the enacted rebuild is exact; moving one of those three sliders imposes its value in every year from the start year, which is how to ask what the FY2022 weights would have done earlier.
  • Full-formula presets. "Full formula, no phase-in, hold-harmless" pays every underfunded town its formula amount from the start year and lets no town fall below its prior grant; it is the approach the state adopted for FY2026, applied earlier. "Full formula, no phase-in, no hold-harmless" pays every town exactly its formula amount, so towns above their formula amount lose money from the first year; it costs less in total and redistributes rather than adds, which is why it produces negative simulated effects in some low-need towns.
  • Statutory basis. Every year's phase-in and hold-harmless rule was checked against CGS §10-262h as codified and the Office of Legislative Research analyses of the acts that set it (PA 17-2 June Special Session §§225-230; PA 21-2 June Special Session §§384-386; PA 23-204 §356; PA 25-168 §323). The statute confirms the two rules that had first been inferred from the grant files: overfunded towns are held at their prior amount from FY2022 (PA 21-2 suspended the decreases, PA 23-204 and PA 25-168 kept extending the pause, so reductions now begin in FY2028), and the FY2017 floor for Alliance districts appears in the FY2024 and later paragraphs added by PA 23-204 ("the greater of" the calculated amount, the base grant and the previous year), not in the FY2023 paragraph, which is why Plainfield's FY2023 grant sits below its FY2017 grant. One clause is not modeled: for FY2019 through FY2021 an Alliance district whose fully funded grant fell below its FY2017 base grant was entitled to exactly the base grant; the engine floors such towns at their prior grant instead, which differs only where a district's prior grant exceeded its base, and CSDE's payments match the engine in every such case except Windsor Locks in FY2021. The year-by-year citations are in the ecs_formula_parameters dataset (phase_in_statute) and below.
  • Massachusetts Chapter 70 rule (preset). Replaces the ECS formula with Massachusetts' aid rule applied to each town from the start year, chaining on the town's prior aid (its ECS grant before the start year). Foundation budget: DESE's FY2025 per-pupil rates for the eleven cost categories, applied to the town's ECS resident students split into pre-K, kindergarten, grades 1-5, 6-8 and 9-12 using the grade mix of its local district plus its share of any regional district (NCES Common Core of Data), with pre-K at the pre-school rate; assumed special-education enrollment of 3.93% in-district and 1% tuitioned-out of K-12 pupils at DESE's rates; English-learner increments by band, allocating the ECS English-learner count across bands in proportion to enrollment; the low-income increment for the town's concentration group (twelve groups by low-income share, DESE FY2025 tiers) applied to the chosen low-income count: ECS FRPL-eligible students (the same 185%-of-poverty line Massachusetts uses, but identified partly through meal applications), free-lunch-eligible students only (EdSight; 130% of poverty or direct certification, a lower bound), or directly certified students (CSDE's Community Eligibility Provision identified-student percentage applied to resident students: SNAP, Temporary Family Assistance, Medicaid, foster care, homeless and migrant matches, the closest analogue to Massachusetts' administrative matching; CEP data exist for 2023-24 to 2025-26 and the nearest year is used elsewhere); and a wage adjustment factor of one plus a third of the county's excess of average pay over the state average (BLS QCEW; never below one; Fairfield County is the only county above the state average), applied to every category except materials, benefits and special-education tuition. Rates are FY2025 dollars moved to other years by the chosen index with the 4.5% annual cap of the foundation inflation index. Required local contribution: the steady-state target, the lesser of a uniform property rate on the town's equalized net grand list (OPM, latest grand list in the ECS window) plus a uniform income rate on its aggregate household income (ACS 5-year table B19025, vintage t-4) and 82.5% of its foundation budget; the two rates are solved each year so that property and income yield equal amounts and capped targets sum to the chosen statewide local share (59% in Massachusetts). Aid = foundation budget minus required contribution, never less than the prior year's aid plus the minimum per-pupil increase. Not modeled: Massachusetts' multi-year transition of contributions toward target (municipal revenue growth factors and effort increments), vocational enrollment (Connecticut's technical high schools are state-run and outside the resident count), the net-school-spending enforcement, and any local response. Massachusetts identifies low income through administrative matches (SNAP, TAFDC, MassHealth, foster care, homelessness) at the same 185%-of-poverty line as FRPL; statewide the shares are close (45.6% in Massachusetts, 43% FRPL in Connecticut), but towns can change concentration tier depending on the basis chosen. See the assumptions document in the repository for the full list.
  • Start year. Years before the chosen start year follow enacted policy exactly; from the start year on, the scenario applies and chains forward on its own simulated prior-year grant.
  • Per-student chart. The student measure is the ECS resident-student count (students the town is fiscally responsible for on October 1), because it is the denominator the formula itself uses and it exists for every town and year; it excludes charter and technical-high-school students. The low-income share is FRPL-eligible students over resident students from the same worksheet. The fitted line is ordinary least squares across the 169 towns, unweighted unless the box is ticked; the slope is reported per 10-percentage-point rise in the FRPL share.
  • Total spending per student (NCEP). The second vertical-axis option uses CSDE's net current expenditures per pupil: all-source current spending (net of tuition revenue, capital, debt service and reimbursable transportation) divided by average daily membership. ADM is the resident-student count, the same count the ECS formula uses, so the denominator is consistent in construction. The two are one October apart: the FY2025 NCEP divides FY2025 spending by the October 2024 count, while the FY2025 ECS grant was computed on the October 2023 count (FY2025 ADM equals the FY2026 ECS resident count to the cent for 135 of 148 towns). Under a scenario, the town's spending is moved by its ECS change one for one, which assumes no offsetting local tax response. NCEP exists only for the 148 towns with a local board (regional-district members carry their tuition payments, not the region's spending) and is published through FY2025; FY2023 onward comes from CSDE's reports as archived by the School and State Finance Project, with the current year from CSDE's own file.
  • Achievement (SEDA). Scores are the Stanford Education Data Archive 2025.2 administrative-district estimates (Reardon, Fahle, Ho, Shear, Saliba, Min, Shim and Kalogrides, 2026), grades 3-8 pooled, empirical-Bayes means on the grade-cohort-standardized (GCS) scale, minus the grade center of 5.5. On that scale the national average of the 2009-2015 cohorts in grade g equals g, so the value plotted is grade levels above or below that national benchmark, not the 2025 national average. SEDA advises the GCS scale for reporting rather than research use. Town boards are matched by name; the 21 towns whose grades 3-8 are run by a K-12 regional district (Regions 10, 12-18 and 20; Region 6 before 2024-25) carry the region's score and are drawn hollow; small districts that SEDA suppresses have no score; in statewide and tercile averages each member town of a regional district is given the region's tests in proportion to its resident students, so a region is not counted once per member. Charter and magnet districts are not assigned to towns. When a scenario is active the chart also draws scenario points and a second fitted line: each town's observed score plus the simulated change from the test-score simulation below, so the slope change reflects the simulated policy effect, not an estimated relationship.
  • Test-score simulation. Follows the simulation instructions of 2026-09-14. The shock is the scenario's ECS entitlement minus enacted policy, per ECS resident student, in each fiscal year from the chosen start year, deflated to 2018 dollars with CPI-U and treated as an operating (noncapital) spending increase spent one for one. Effect size β = 0.0343 student-level SD per $1,000 per pupil at four years of exposure, the noncapital marginal effect in Jackson & Mackevicius (2024, AEJ: Applied 16(1), Table 3 col 2). Exposure assumes no churn since kindergarten: min(policy year, grade + 1); the dose rises linearly to four years and is flat after, so with a phased-in shock the effect is β/4 times the sum of the last min(exposure, 4) years' increases. SD are converted to grade levels with k taken from SEDA's own scale definitions: k = national NAEP SD for the grade and subject ÷ the per-grade NAEP growth for the subject, from Table 9 of the SEDA 2025.2 documentation averaged over the four reference cohorts (grade 3 math 2.70 to grade 8 math 3.71, reading 3.30–3.46; SEDA's rule of thumb is about 3). The slope of GCS on CS across all U.S. districts reproduces these to within 0.006. Baselines and weights are SEDA 2025.2 grade-by-subject means averaged over spring 2023–2025. Because the dashboard runs only through FY2025, no cohort reaches the four-year plateau. Statewide and tercile figures are test-weighted; terciles are by FY2025 FRPL share. The Option A sensitivity scales β by the district's FRPL share using the low-income (0.0312) and non-low-income (0.0161) effects relative to the 0.0359 constant (Table 3 col 3) and is not normalized to average one. Dose shape: the linear ramp to four years with a plateau after is a modeling choice, not a result of the paper; the "linear score growth" box removes the plateau so effects keep accumulating with every year of exposure, which matters once cohorts have five to seven policy years. Pass-through: the shock is the ECS change multiplied by a pass-through rate, 100% in Alliance and Priority School Districts (whose Alliance portion must reach the board) and the slider value elsewhere; ECS is paid to the town, and outside the Alliance requirement nothing obliges a town to add an increase to the school budget rather than use it for tax relief, so 100% is an upper bound. In-district and out-of-district students: the ECS change is divided by the town's resident students, which include children the town pays to educate elsewhere (magnets, charters, Open Choice, agricultural science centers), and the resulting per-pupil change is applied to the district's own tested students. The model therefore assumes the town spreads an ECS change evenly across all resident students, in-district and out, so its own schools receive the per-resident change for the children they teach. In Hartford, New Haven and Bridgeport, where a large share of residents attend magnets and charters, this understates the per-pupil change if the town keeps the money for its own schools and overstates it if the money follows students out of district; the data here cannot separate the two. This is a partial-equilibrium projection with no behavioral responses beyond those embedded in the reduced-form estimates, and no uncertainty is shown. A Python reference implementation with unit tests, an aggregation check of the grade-level files against the pooled estimates, and a log of every run live in the working repository under output/ecs/dashboard/sim and output/ecs/sim.
  • Average household income is an option, not the statute. It substitutes the ACS 5-year mean household income (subject table S1901, same vintage as the median the state used) for the town's median, and replaces the statewide median of medians with the statewide median of town means, so the wealth threshold is rebuilt the same way. Towns with no published mean keep their median.
  • Inflation preset grows the $11,525 foundation from calendar 2013 (the year it was set for FY2014) by the chosen index's annual average for the calendar year before each fiscal year; FY2027 uses the last complete year available. The index is CPI-U by default or, at the user's choice, the Employment Cost Index for state and local government workers (total compensation, BLS series CIU3010000000000I), which better tracks the cost of keeping the same staff and has outpaced consumer prices since 2021 (FY2025 foundation $15,937 under the ECI against $15,520 under CPI-U). The $13,087 preset applies a 2027 figure to every year, which overstates it for earlier years.
  • Worksheets. FY2019–FY2021 and FY2023–FY2027 use the official OFA/CSDE calculation shells (received from the state in September 2026); FY2022 uses the School and State Finance Project's copy of that year's shell, which matches CSDE's grants for 158 of 169 towns to the dollar and every town within $50. FY2020 and FY2021 do not reproduce for nine to eleven Alliance towns whose FY2020 grant CSDE recalculated with revised Public Investment Community rankings; the worksheet carries the earlier ranking and the same dollar gap persists into FY2021. FY2027 awaits CSDE's final calculation. The reproduction table shows where each of these matters.
  • Not modeled: minimum budget requirement interactions, Alliance set-asides (which change who receives the money, not the entitlement), supplemental education aid, special-education adjustments, and any statutory earmark or one-off grant outside the formula.
  • Real dollars. Every dollar figure on the page is in constant 2025 dollars: fiscal year t is deflated with the CPI-U annual average for calendar year t, the convention of the public data release. FY2026 and FY2027 use the 2025 index because later annual averages are not yet complete, so they appear at 2025 prices. The formula itself runs in nominal dollars, so the foundation slider and the $11,525 and $13,087 presets are nominal amounts; the inflation preset raises the nominal foundation with CPI-U from 2013.
  • Data. Amounts: CSDE Bureau of Fiscal Services entitlement files. Inputs: the official Office of Fiscal Analysis / CSDE calculation shells for FY2019–FY2021 and FY2023–FY2027 and the School and State Finance Project's copy for FY2022, validated against OPM's equalized net grand list, Census ACS median income and CSDE's published entitlements. The series starts in FY2019, the first year grants were computed by the current formula (PA 17-2); FY2018 grants were legislated amounts with holdbacks and enter only through the CSDE published line and the FY2017 base.
Data assembled September 2026 from the public-CT-ed-data release (datasets town_year_ecs_entitlement, town_year_ecs_inputs, ecs_formula_parameters); formula inputs from the official OFA/CSDE calculation shells. Town boundaries: Census Bureau cartographic boundary files, 2023.
